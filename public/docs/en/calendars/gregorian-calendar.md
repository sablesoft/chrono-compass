# Gregorian Calendar

Browse Gregorian months on the same calendar surface as Epoch Calendar, with the same astronomical event layers.

## Finding a date

- **Today** opens the current month in the selected location’s time zone.
- **← / →** move to the previous or next month, including across year boundaries.
- Choose **Era, Year and Month**, then press **Go**. CE and BCE years are numbered from 1; there is no displayed year zero.
- Choose **Week starts: Mon or Sun** to arrange the weekday columns. Sunday is the default. The grid updates immediately and your choice is saved on this device. Each month has its usual Gregorian length, including February 29 in leap years.
- Dates before the historical adoption of the Gregorian calendar use its rules extended backward: the **proleptic Gregorian calendar**.

## Display options

Enable **Epoch dates** to show the corresponding Epoch Month and day in each cell. **M10 · 13**, for example, means Month 10, day 13. Free days appear as **S** or **S1–S4**. Hover a cell for the full Epoch coordinate; the range above the grid shows the coordinates of the first and last days.

The grid uses neutral backgrounds, with each date aligned to its weekday column.

The same wheel layers are available in both calendars:

- **Season events:** ☀ plus E, N, W or S at the upper right for equinoxes and solstices.
- **Sun Bind: Earth:** 🌍 plus E, N, W or S at the lower right for Earth–Sun distance events.
- **Lunar phases:** phase icons at the upper left.

Hover an event marker for its full name and calculated moment in local time and UTC. Events below the grid are grouped by layer. The layers currently support Gregorian years 1001–2999; their times inherit the approximations of the corresponding wheel solvers.

Wheel selections are shared between the two calendars. The **Epoch dates** preference is saved separately from Epoch Calendar’s **Gregorian dates** preference.

Calendar arithmetic and installed offline resources work without an internet connection. Today follows the device clock and the selected time zone; changing location can move an event to an adjacent day.
