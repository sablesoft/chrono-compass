# Lunar Draconic Cycle

## Overview

The **lunar draconic cycle** describes the periodic motion of the Moon relative to its **orbital nodes**.

It is defined by the rotation of the Moon’s orbit around the Earth and determines when the Moon crosses:
- the **ascending node** (moving north of the ecliptic),
- the **descending node** (moving south of the ecliptic).

The draconic cycle is the **key cycle governing eclipses**.

Without node alignment:
- solar eclipses cannot occur,
- lunar eclipses cannot occur.

In ChronoCompass, the draconic cycle is treated as a **nodal and alignment cycle**, focused on geometric conditions rather than visibility or distance.

---

## Cycle Definition

- Average duration: **~27.21222 days**
- Cycle type: **Moon–Earth–Sun orbital geometry**
- Reference frame: Moon’s orbital plane relative to the ecliptic

Astronomically, the draconic cycle runs from **node crossing to the same node crossing**.
In ChronoCompass, however, **East (E)** is always treated as the formal start and end of the cycle, preserving a unified directional structure across all wheels.

---

## Directions and Anchors

The lunar draconic wheel uses the following primary anchors:

| Direction | Astronomical Meaning       |
|-----------|----------------------------|
| **E**     | Ascending Node             |
| **N**     | Maximum northern deviation |
| **W**     | Descending Node            |
| **S**     | Maximum southern deviation |
| **E+**    | Next Ascending Node        |

Intermediate directions represent gradual motion between nodal extremes.

---

## Houses

- The wheel is divided into **16 houses**, centered on the 16 directions.
- Houses represent **degrees of nodal proximity**, not equal time slices.
- Temporal length of houses may vary slightly due to orbital mechanics.

Houses indicate how close the Moon is to the **eclipse-capable geometry**.

---

## Meaning of Directions

### East (E) — Ascending Node

- The Moon crosses the ecliptic plane moving **northward**.
- The Moon’s orbit intersects the Earth–Sun plane.

From a geometric perspective:

- The Moon is exactly aligned with the ecliptic.
- Eclipse conditions become **possible**, but not guaranteed.

East represents:
- **entry into eclipse-capable geometry**,
- initiation of nodal alignment,
- structural beginning of the draconic cycle.

---

### West (W) — Descending Node

- The Moon crosses the ecliptic plane moving **southward**.
- This is the second eclipse-capable crossing.

West represents:
- completion of one nodal passage,
- transition out of one alignment regime into another,
- the second gateway for eclipse geometry.

---

### Clarification: Meaning of North (N) and South (S)

In the lunar draconic wheel, **North (N)** and **South (S)** do **not** represent the physically highest or lowest points of the Moon’s orbit in space.

They also do **not** directly represent the maximum numerical value of orbital inclination.

Instead:

> **N and S mark moments of maximal temporal distance from the orbital nodes within the draconic cycle.**

That is:

- **E / W** correspond to the **ascending and descending nodes**,  
  where the Moon crosses the ecliptic plane.
- **N / S** correspond to moments that lie approximately halfway in time between node crossings.

These points represent:
- maximum separation *in phase* from the nodes,
- not guaranteed maximum separation *in spatial height*.

---

### Why This Distinction Matters

The Moon’s actual spatial height above or below the ecliptic depends on:
- orbital inclination,
- argument of perigee,
- precession of the apsidal line,
- and long-term perturbations.

These parameters evolve with **different periods** than the draconic month.

As a result:
- the moment of greatest angular distance from the ecliptic plane,
- and the moment of greatest spatial “vertical” excursion,
  do **not remain phase-locked** to the draconic cycle.

ChronoCompass therefore treats the draconic cycle strictly as a **node-relative cycle**.

---

## Eclipse Logic

Eclipses require **simultaneous alignment** of multiple cycles:

- **Synodic cycle** → phase (New or Full Moon),
- **Draconic cycle** → proximity to nodes,
- **Anomalistic cycle** → distance (intensity modulation).

In ChronoCompass:

- Eclipses become possible when synodic South (New Moon) or North (Full Moon)
  aligns with draconic East or West (nodes).
- Exact eclipses occur only within **narrow nodal houses** around E and W.

The draconic cycle therefore defines **where eclipses may occur**, not **when** they must.

---

## Interaction with Other Cycles

The lunar draconic cycle gains full meaning only in combination with:

- **Lunar Synodic Cycle**  
  (phase alignment: New / Full Moon)

- **Lunar Anomalistic Cycle**  
  (distance-dependent eclipse magnitude)

- **Solar Tropical Cycle**  
  (seasonal geometry of Earth–Sun distance)

This interaction produces:
- eclipse seasons,
- periodic clustering of eclipses,
- long-term drift of eclipse geometry.

---

## Practical Applications

The lunar draconic wheel is useful for:

- identifying **eclipse windows**,
- distinguishing solar vs lunar eclipse potential,
- understanding why most New or Full Moons do *not* produce eclipses,
- visualizing nodal drift over time,
- educational explanation of orbital inclination.

---

### Conceptual Interpretation in ChronoCompass

Within the Unified Compass Model:

- **Draconic N / S** represent:
    - maximal *node-distance in time*,
    - minimal probability of eclipse alignment,
    - phases where the Moon is most “detached” from the ecliptic crossing geometry.

In the unified system:
- synodic → phase,
- anomalistic → intensity,
- draconic → possibility.

They answer the question:

> *How far (in phase) is the Moon from any node crossing right now?*

They do **not** answer:

> *How high or low is the Moon in absolute 3D space?*

This separation preserves physical correctness and avoids mixing distinct orbital phenomena into a single wheel.

## Summary

- The lunar draconic cycle governs **orbital node alignment**.
- East and West correspond to **ascending and descending nodes**.
- North and South represent **maximum deviation from the ecliptic**.
- Only near nodes can eclipses occur.
- Houses describe degrees of eclipse readiness, not equal time divisions.

ChronoCompass treats the draconic cycle as the **geometric gatekeeper** of eclipses within the unified compass model.
