# Plato Cycle (Axial Precession)

## Overview

The **Plato cycle** (also known as the **Platonic Year**) describes the slow **precession of Earth’s rotational axis** in inertial space.

As a result of gravitational torques exerted primarily by the Sun and Moon, Earth’s axis traces a long conical motion around the **normal of the ecliptic plane**.

Unlike orbital or illumination-based cycles, the Plato cycle is governed purely by **orientation**, not by distance, seasons, or apparent motion of the Sun or Moon.

In ChronoCompass, the Plato cycle is treated as a **deep-time orientation cycle**.

---

## Cycle Definition

- Average duration: **~25,772 years**
- Cycle type: **axial precession**
- Reference frame: **inertial (extrinsic)**

Astronomically, the precessional cycle is continuous and does not possess natural extrema comparable to perihelion or solstice.

ChronoCompass therefore defines a **formal directional structure** to integrate the cycle coherently with other wheels.

---

## Directions and Anchors

The Plato wheel uses the following anchors:

| Direction | Meaning                                              |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------------|
| **E**     | Formal start of the precessional cycle               |
| **N**     | Quarter phase of axial rotation                      |
| **W**     | Half phase of axial rotation                         |
| **S**     | Alignment of Earth’s axis toward the Galactic Center |
| **E+**    | Completion of the cycle                              |

Key notes:
- The cycle has **one physically meaningful focal orientation**.
- Other directions represent **uniform phase progression**, not physical extrema.

---

## Galactic Reference

The Plato cycle is anchored to the **direction of the Galactic Center**, expressed in ICRS/J2000 coordinates.

In ChronoCompass, the Galactic Center is used as a **stable inertial reference** on multi-millennial timescales:
- it is external to Earth–Sun–Moon dynamics,
- it does not depend on seasons, orbital eccentricity, or observer location,
- and it changes negligibly over the duration of a single precessional cycle.

This choice provides a consistent directional target for an otherwise continuous rotational process.

---

## Meaning of South (S)

### South (S) — Galactic Alignment

- Earth’s rotational axis is oriented closest to the **Galactic Center**.
- This moment represents **maximum inertial alignment**, not a force peak.

From a structural perspective:

- The axis is not “at rest” or “at a maximum”, but **oriented** toward a fixed external direction.
- The event is directional rather than dynamic.

In ChronoCompass symbolism:

- **South** represents depth, inward orientation, and structural focus.
- It is consistently associated with **alignment**, compression, or binding rather than expansion.

For this reason:
- the Galactic Center alignment is mapped to **S**,
- while **E** remains the formal cycle boundary to preserve uniformity across all wheels.

---

## East (E), North (N), and West (W)

- These directions represent **pure phase divisions** of the precessional cycle.
- They do not correspond to physical extrema or changes in speed.
- Precession is modeled as **uniform angular motion**.

In ChronoCompass:
- **East (E)** marks the beginning and completion of the cycle,
- ensuring consistency with other wheels, even when no natural “start” exists.

---

## Houses

- The wheel is divided into **16 houses**, centered on the 16 directions.
- Houses represent **gradual change in axial orientation**.
- House durations are treated as **equal**, reflecting the near-uniform rate of precession.
