# Lunar Anomalistic Cycle

## Overview

The **lunar anomalistic cycle** describes the periodic change in the **distance between the Moon and the Earth**.

It is defined by the Moon’s motion along its **elliptical orbit**, focusing on the repetition of:
- **Perigee** (minimum distance),
- **Apogee** (maximum distance).

This cycle governs variations in:
- lunar gravitational influence,
- tidal strength,
- apparent angular size of the Moon.

In ChronoCompass, the anomalistic cycle is treated as a **distance-and-gravity cycle**, complementing the phase-based synodic cycle.

---

## Cycle Definition

- Average duration: **~27.55455 days**
- Cycle type: **Earth–Moon distance variation**
- Reference frame: Earth-centered

Astronomically, the anomalistic cycle runs from **Perigee to Perigee**.
In ChronoCompass, however, **East (E)** is always treated as the formal start and end of the cycle, ensuring a unified directional structure across all wheels.

---

## Directions and Anchors

The lunar anomalistic wheel uses the following primary anchors:

| Direction | Astronomical Meaning            |
|-----------|---------------------------------|
| **E**     |  Midpoint (distance increasing) |
| **N**     | Apogee (maximum distance)       |
| **W**     | Midpoint (distance decreasing)  |
| **S**     | Perigee (minimum distance)      |
| **E+**    | Next midpoint                   |

Intermediate directions represent continuous change in distance between these extrema.

---

## Houses

- The wheel is divided into **16 houses**, centered on the 16 directions.
- Houses represent **distance regimes**, not illumination or visibility.
- Temporal house length is not forced to be equal, but is derived from the true orbital geometry.

Houses describe **qualitative gravitational states** of the Earth–Moon system.

---

## Meaning of Directions

### South (S) — Perigee

- The Moon is at its **minimum distance** from Earth.
- Lunar gravitational influence reaches its **maximum**.

From a gravitational perspective:

- The Moon’s gravitational vector acting on Earth is strongest.
- Tidal forces are amplified.
- Any alignment with the Sun (e.g. New or Full Moon) produces enhanced effects.

South therefore represents:
- **maximum hidden gravitational intensity**,
- compression,
- concentration of influence.

This is why Perigee is associated with:
- stronger tides,
- so-called “supermoons” when combined with the synodic cycle,
- heightened sensitivity of Earth–Moon interactions.

---

### North (N) — Apogee

- The Moon is at its **maximum distance** from Earth.
- Lunar gravitational influence is weakest.

From a gravitational perspective:

- The Moon’s pull on Earth is reduced.
- Tidal amplitudes are diminished.

North represents:
- **minimum gravitational binding**,
- relative freedom of motion,
- expansion and dispersion within the cycle.

In contrast to South, North expresses:
- release,
- lightness,
- and reduced constraint.

---

### East (E) and West (W)

- Transitional points between Perigee and Apogee.
- Moments where the rate of distance change is balanced.

From a gravitational perspective:

- The system transitions between strengthening and weakening lunar influence.
- No extreme dominates.

East and West represent:
- turning points,
- redistribution of gravitational influence,
- dynamic equilibrium.

In ChronoCompass:
- **East always marks both the beginning and the completion of the anomalistic cycle**, preserving structural consistency across all wheels.

---

## Interaction with Other Cycles

The lunar anomalistic cycle is especially significant when combined with:

- **Lunar Synodic Cycle**  
  (phase and illumination)

- **Lunar Draconic Cycle**  
  (orbital nodes and eclipses)

Examples:
- **Perigee + Full Moon** → enhanced tidal effects (“supermoon”),
- **Perigee + New Moon** → strong spring tides,
- **Perigee + Node alignment** → intensified eclipses.

The anomalistic cycle modulates the *strength* of effects produced by other lunar cycles.

---

## Practical Applications

The lunar anomalistic wheel is useful for:

- estimating variations in tidal strength,
- contextualizing “supermoon” events,
- refining eclipse impact analysis,
- studying gravitational modulation over time,
- educational visualization of elliptical orbits.

---

## Conceptual Role in ChronoCompass

The anomalistic cycle answers the question:
> *How strong is the Moon’s gravitational influence right now?*

While the synodic cycle defines *phase*, the anomalistic cycle defines *intensity*.

Together, they form a complementary pair:
- synodic → alignment and visibility,
- anomalistic → distance and gravitational strength.

## Summary

- The lunar anomalistic cycle is a cycle of **distance extremes**.
- South corresponds to **maximum gravitational influence (Perigee)**.
- North corresponds to **minimum gravitational influence (Apogee)**.
- East and West are **transitional equilibrium points**.
- Houses represent qualitative gravitational states, not equal time slices.

ChronoCompass treats the lunar anomalistic cycle as a **gravitational modulation layer** within the unified compass framework.
