# Lunar Synodic Cycle

## Overview

The **lunar synodic cycle** describes the repeating sequence of **Moon phases** as observed from Earth.

It is defined by the changing angular relationship between:
- the Sun,
- the Earth,
- and the Moon.

The synodic cycle governs:
- New Moon,
- First Quarter,
- Full Moon,
- Last Quarter,

and is the most familiar lunar cycle in human culture, calendars, and observation.

---

## Cycle Definition

- Average duration: **~29.53059 days**
- Cycle type: **Sun–Earth–Moon alignment**
- Reference frame: observer on Earth

Astronomically, the cycle runs from **New Moon to New Moon**.
In ChronoCompass, however, **East (E)** is always treated as the formal start and end of the cycle, ensuring a unified directional structure across all wheels.

---

## Directions and Anchors

The lunar synodic wheel uses the following primary anchors:

| Direction | Astronomical Meaning |
|-----------|----------------------|
| **E**     | First Quarter        |
| **N**     | Full Moon            |
| **W**     | Last Quarter         |
| **S**     | New Moon             |
| **E+**    | Next First Quarter   |

Intermediate directions represent waxing and waning sub-phases.

---

## Houses

- The wheel is divided into **16 houses**, centered on the 16 directions.
- Houses are defined by phase progression, not by equal temporal length.
- Waxing and waning halves of the cycle are structurally symmetric, but not forced into exact time equality.

Houses represent **qualitative lunar states**, not merely percentages of illumination.

---

## Meaning of Directions

### South (S) — New Moon

- The Moon lies approximately between the Earth and the Sun.
- The Moon is visually invisible from Earth.

From a gravitational perspective:

- The gravitational vectors of the **Moon and the Sun are aligned**.
- Both act in roughly the same direction relative to the Earth.
- This produces **maximum combined tidal and gravitational influence**.

South therefore represents:
- a point of **maximum hidden gravitational tension**,
- alignment,
- compression,
- and potential.

This is why New Moon is associated with:
- strong tides,
- beginnings,
- and latent energy rather than visible manifestation.

---

### North (N) — Full Moon

- The Moon is opposite the Sun relative to Earth.
- Maximum lunar illumination.

From a gravitational perspective:

- The Moon and Sun pull on the Earth from **opposite directions**.
- Their gravitational vectors partially **counteract each other**.
- This creates a state of **dynamic balance rather than reinforcement**.

North represents:
- maximum visibility,
- exposure,
- release,
- and distribution of influence.

Although tidal forces remain strong, the system is less compressed than at New Moon.

---

### East (E) — First Quarter
### West (W) — Last Quarter

- Transitional phases between alignment and opposition.
- The Moon–Sun angle is approximately 90°.

From a gravitational perspective:

- Lunar and solar gravitational vectors are **orthogonal**.
- Neither reinforces nor directly cancels the other.
- The system passes through a state of **relative equilibrium and change**.

East and West represent:
- turning points,
- phase inversion,
- and redistribution of influence.

In ChronoCompass:
- **East always marks both the beginning and the completion of the synodic cycle**,
  providing a consistent structural anchor across all wheels.

## Interaction with Other Cycles

The synodic cycle gains deeper meaning when observed together with:

- **Lunar Anomalistic Cycle**  
  (distance-dependent gravitational strength)

- **Lunar Draconic Cycle**  
  (node alignment and eclipse probability)

- **Diurnal Cycle**  
  (local manifestation of lunar effects)

For example:
- New Moon near **Perigee** amplifies gravitational effects (supermoon),
- New or Full Moon near **nodes** enables eclipses.

---

## Practical Applications

The lunar synodic wheel is useful for:

- understanding Moon phases beyond simple illumination,
- predicting **spring and neap tides**,
- contextualizing eclipses when combined with draconic data,
- analyzing resonance with other cycles,
- educational and cultural interpretation of lunar time.

---

## Conceptual Role in ChronoCompass

The synodic cycle establishes a **baseline lunar rhythm**.

It answers the question:
> *How is the Moon positioned relative to the Sun and the Earth right now?*

Other lunar cycles refine this answer, but the synodic cycle provides the primary phase structure upon which they build.

## Summary

- The lunar synodic cycle is a cycle of **alignment and opposition**.
- South corresponds to **maximum combined gravitational influence**.
- North corresponds to **maximum visibility and balanced forces**.
- East and West are **transitional equilibria**.
- Houses represent qualitative lunar states, not equal time slices.

ChronoCompass treats the synodic cycle as a **structural foundation**, not just a phase calendar.
